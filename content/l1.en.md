## Core Notes

### Linking (catenation)

In real speech, word boundaries disappear — you hear **chunks**, not single words.

- **Consonant + vowel:** *pick it up* → "pi-ki-tup"; *turn it off* → "tur-ni-toff"
- **Vowel + vowel (glide inserted):** *go on* → "go-**w**-on"; *I agree* → "I-**j**-agree"
- **Linking /r/ (BrE):** *far away* → "fa-**r**-away"
- **Same consonant (merged):** *black coffee* → one long /k/

### Weak forms: of / to / and

Function words (articles, prepositions, conjunctions, auxiliaries, pronouns) reduce to **schwa /ə/** when unstressed. Content words keep their stress.

| Word | Strong | Weak | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| of | /ɒv/ | /ə/ | a lot of → "lotta" |
| to | /tuː/ | /tə/ | want to → *wanna* |
| and | /ænd/ | /ən/ | fish and chips → "fish 'n' chips" |
| can | /kæn/ | /kən/ | I can swim |

**Trap:** weak *can* /kən/ vs strong *can't* /kɑːnt/ — tell them apart by vowel and stress, not the /t/.

### Elision & assimilation

- **Elision (dropped sounds):** *next day* → "nex' day"; *tell him* → "tell 'im"; *comfortable* → "comf'table"
- **Assimilation (changed sounds):** *did you* → /ˈdɪdʒə/; *don't you* → "dontcha"; *ten pounds* → "tem pounds"

### Graded materials

Pick material just above your level (i+1): graded or slow news → learner podcasts → authentic TED and interviews. Listen first for gist, then check the transcript — the gaps are almost always linking, weak forms, or elision.

### Shadowing

Listen and repeat about one second behind, copying **rhythm, linking, and intonation** — not just words. Steps: whole listen → read transcript → shadow with text → shadow without → record and compare. Use 20–30 second clips.

### Summary

Chunks, not words. Function words go to /ə/. *did you* → *dʒə*. Transcript plus shadowing trains ear and mouth together.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** You hear /ˌwɒtʃəˈduːɪŋ/. → **What are you doing?** (*are you* weakens and assimilates to "chə")

**Example 2.** Restore: /aɪ ˈwɒnə ˈɡræb ə ˈkɒfi/ → **I want to grab a coffee.**

**Example 3.** Fill the weak words: I've got ___ ___ ___ work ("a lotta"). → **a lot of**

**Example 4.** Restore: "Whaddaya wanna do?" → **What do you want to do?**

**Example 5.** Assimilation: *would you* → **/ˈwʊdʒə/** ("wouldja")

## Exercises

1. Give one example of each linking type.
2. Write the weak-form sounds of of / to / and.
3. How do you tell *can* from *can't* by ear?
4. Restore: /ˈɡɪmi ə ˈsekən/.
5. What does *did you* become after assimilation?
6. How is shadowing different from word-by-word translation?

**Answer key.**

1) C+V pick it up; V+V go-w-on; linking /r/ far away; same consonant black coffee. 2) /ə/, /tə/, /ən/. 3) *can* is weak /kən/ and unstressed; *can't* is stressed with a clear vowel. 4) Give me a second. 5) /ˈdɪdʒə/. 6) Shadowing copies whole rhythm and linking (ear plus mouth); translation handles isolated words and lags behind speech.
