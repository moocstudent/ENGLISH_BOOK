## Core Notes

### Features of academic text

Follow the **argument**, not the vocabulary. Look for **claims** and **evidence**.

- **Nominalization:** actions become nouns (*they decided* → *the decision*); denser sentences.
- **Objective / passive:** *It was found that…*
- **Hedging:** *may, tends to, suggests, is likely* — few absolutes.
- **Connectors:** *however, moreover, therefore* are the road signs of the argument.

For long sentences, find the **subject-verb-object core** first; bracket the clauses as extra detail.

### Reading an abstract (IMRaD)

| Part | Question | Signals |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | what problem? | aims to, examines |
| Methods | how done? | using, we analyzed |
| Results | what found? | found, showed |
| Discussion | so what? | suggests, conclude |

Read the **last sentence first** (usually the conclusion), then decide if the full text is worth it.

### Argument schema

Argument = **thesis + supporting claims + evidence**.

- **Thesis:** the core point (end of the intro or abstract).
- **Supporting claims:** one per paragraph (topic sentence).
- **Evidence:** data, examples, citations.

If you can restate the thesis in one sentence, you understood it.

### Citation and paraphrase

Know **who is speaking**.

- **Direct quote:** in quotation marks.
- **Paraphrase:** *X argues that…*, *According to Y…*
- **Reporting verbs leak attitude:** *notes, shows* (neutral) vs *claims, asserts* (reserved) vs *overlooks, fails to* (critical).

*However, this view ignores…* signals that the author disagrees with the cited view.

### Skimming a whole chapter

1. Read objectives and subheadings for a frame.
2. First paragraph, plus the first sentence of each section.
3. Bold terms, figure captions, chapter summary.
4. Restate the structure, then reread hard parts closely.

Take **Cornell notes**: cue words left, notes right, one-line summary at the bottom.

### Summary

Follow the argument; read abstracts by IMRaD; locate thesis → claims → evidence; separate the author's view from cited views.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Abstract ending *"The results suggest that the timing of study matters."* → conclusion = **timing affects memory**.

**Example 2.** *The theory, which was first proposed decades ago, still fails to explain the anomaly.* → core: **The theory still fails to explain the anomaly.**

**Example 3.** *Smith argues that the policy works. However, this claim overlooks the cost.* → author **disagrees**.

**Example 4.** *The findings are tentative and require further study.* → tentative ≈ **provisional, uncertain**.

**Example 5.** *This essay argues the opposite: the problem is not our devices but how we use them.* → thesis = **usage, not devices**.

## Exercises

1. What does each IMRaD part answer?
2. Which sentence of an abstract do you read first?
3. Core of *The report, based on limited data, nonetheless reaches a strong conclusion.*
4. *Jones claims X, but the evidence is thin.* — does the author accept X?
5. What attitude does *overlooks* signal?

**Answer key.** 1) problem, method, results, meaning. 2) the conclusion (last sentence). 3) *The report nonetheless reaches a strong conclusion.* 4) No. 5) critical.
