## Core Notes

### Email components

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Subject | specific, short, searchable |
| Greeting | Dear Ms. Lee, / Hi Tom, |
| Opening | I am writing to … |
| Body | details, one topic per paragraph |
| Action | clear request + deadline |
| Sign-off | Kind regards, + name |

Greeting to close (British): *Dear Sir or Madam* → **Yours faithfully**; *Dear Ms. Lee* → **Yours sincerely**; safe all-purpose → **Kind regards**.

### Requests, apologies, follow-ups

| Purpose | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Request | Could you please …?; I would appreciate it if you could … |
| Apology | I apologise for …; Please accept my apologies for … |
| Follow-up | I am writing to follow up on …; Just checking the status of … |
| Thanks | Thank you for …; I appreciate your help with … |

### Reports & summaries

Order: **Title → Executive Summary → Background → Findings → Recommendations.**

The executive summary gives the whole story in 3–5 sentences and must stand alone — write it **last**.

### Tone: direct vs indirect

English business writing values politeness and **hedging** (would, could, might, perhaps, I'm afraid). But don't be vague — be clear about what you need and by when.

| Too direct | Better |
|---|---|
| Send me the file. | Could you please send me the file? |
| You are wrong. | I'm afraid there may be a mistake here. |
| I need it now. | Would it be possible to have it by 3 p.m.? |

Formal swaps: get → receive; sorry → apologise; need → require; Can you → Could you.

### Templates vs personalisation

Use a template for structure and sign-offs; personalise the opening line and key details (name, project, last contact). **Reuse the structure, tailor the content.**

### Summary

Six-part email; fixed polite phrases for requests, apologies, and follow-ups; summary first in reports; be polite but clear; template plus a personal touch.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Blunt → polite: *Send me the report today.* → *Could you please send me the report by end of day?*

**Example 2.** *Dear Sir or Madam* pairs with which close? → **Yours faithfully**

**Example 3.** Fill in: *I am writing to ___ ___ on my application.* → **follow up**

**Example 4.** Order ② ④ ③ ① = greeting → purpose → request + deadline → sign-off.

**Example 5.** Memo *"Meeting changed. Be there."* is vague and bossy — add the new time, place, and a polite request to confirm.

## Exercises

1. Name four parts of a business email.
2. Which close suits *Dear Ms. Lee*?
3. Soften *You must reply today.*
4. What does an executive summary do, and when is it written?
5. Write a polite request to extend a deadline.
6. True or false: using a template always sounds insincere.

**Answer key.** (1) subject, greeting, opening, body, action, sign-off; (2) Yours sincerely / Kind regards; (3) *Could you please reply by end of day?*; (4) sums up findings and recommendations in 3–5 sentences, written last; (5) *Would it be possible to extend the deadline to Monday?*; (6) false — personalise the opening and details.
