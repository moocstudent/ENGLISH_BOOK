## Passage

*— Adapted from Frankenstein, Mary Shelley (1818). A public-domain work.*

It was a dreary night in November when my long labour came at last to its end. The rain beat against the windows, and my candle had almost burned away, when by its dying light I saw the creature open its dull yellow eye. It breathed, and a faint movement stirred its limbs.

How can I describe the horror of that moment? I had chosen his features to be beautiful. Beautiful! For nearly two years I had worked, robbing myself of rest and health, with one purpose only — to give life. And now that the work was done, the dream vanished, and a breathless dread filled my heart. The being I had made was a thing I could not bear to look upon.

I rushed from the room and hurried to my bedchamber, unable to calm my mind. At last, worn out, I fell into a troubled sleep, but my dreams were wild and full of fear. I woke in the cold, and by the pale light of the moon I saw the creature standing beside my bed. He drew back the curtain, and his eyes, if eyes they could be called, were fixed upon me. His mouth opened, and he made some low sounds, while one hand reached out as if to hold me.

I did not stay. I ran down the stairs and out into the night, and there I walked until morning, filled with the misery of what I had made.

## Notes

- **Source / author:** *Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus* (1818) is a gothic and science-fiction classic by the British writer Mary Shelley, often seen as an early ancestor of science fiction. This passage is an adaptation, told by Victor in the first person, of the famous scene in which the creature comes to life — not the original text.
- **Main idea:** A scientist labours hard to "create life," yet at the very moment his dream comes true he is overcome by horror and disgust, raising the book's central theme of a creator's responsibility and its cost.
- **Structure / craft:** The scene moves from labour completed, to the creature waking, to a frightened escape, to its return at midnight; images such as the dreary November night, the dying candle, and the "dull yellow eye" build a sense of dread while keeping the telling restrained and never graphic.
- **Tone:** Heavy with regret and fear; the narration stays calm and inward even as the emotion rises step by step.

## Reading Comprehension

1. On what kind of night does the scene take place?
- A) A dreary night in November
- B) A warm summer evening
- C) A bright winter morning
- D) A calm spring afternoon

2. How long had Victor worked to create the creature?
- A) A few days
- B) Nearly two years
- C) About six months
- D) Almost ten years

3. How does Victor feel once the creature comes to life?
- A) Proud and joyful
- B) Curious and calm
- C) Bored and tired
- D) Horrified and full of dread

4. In "a breathless dread filled my heart," the word *dread* most nearly means
- A) mild surprise
- B) deep joy
- C) great fear
- D) quiet hope

5. What does Victor do at the end of the passage?
- A) He speaks kindly to the creature.
- B) He runs away into the night.
- C) He destroys his laboratory.
- D) He falls peacefully asleep.

**Answer Key**

1) **A** — Detail. The opening states it was "a dreary night in November," so B.
2) **B** — Detail. The text says, "For nearly two years I had worked," so C.
3) **D** — Inference / main idea. Once the creature wakes, Victor feels "the horror of that moment" and "a breathless dread filled my heart," and he "could not bear to look upon" his creation — clear horror and disgust, so C.
4) **C** — Vocabulary in context. *dread* means intense fear; the surrounding "horror" and his flight from the room both confirm it, so A.
5) **B** — Craft / plot. At the end he "ran down the stairs and out into the night" — that is, he flees into the darkness, so C.
