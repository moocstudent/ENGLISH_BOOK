## Core Notes

### Layered revision

Work **big to small**: content, then structure, then language, then proofreading. Don't polish sentences you may delete.

| Level | Focus |
|---|---|
| Content | Is the argument complete? |
| Structure | Logical order and clear paragraphs? |
| Language | Word choice, variety, tone |
| Proofread | Spelling, punctuation, agreement |

Leave the draft, then reread it as a fresh reader.

### Coherence: pronouns & substitution

- Keep pronoun reference **clear** — no vague it/this/they.
- Vary repeated nouns with synonyms (the company → the firm → it).
- Put **old information first, new information last**.

> Vague: *The manager told the intern that he had made a mistake.*
> Clear: *The manager admitted that she had made a mistake.*

### Conciseness

| Wordy | Concise |
|---|---|
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| at this point in time | now |
| a large number of | many |
| make a decision | decide |

Cut redundant pairs: each and every, final outcome, repeat again, past history.

### Grammar & punctuation check

- Subject-verb agreement: *The list of items **is** …*
- Comma splice: two full sentences need a period, semicolon, or conjunction.
- Confusables: its/it's, their/there/they're, then/than.
- Keep tense consistent; avoid fragments.

**Read aloud** to catch awkward or overlong sentences. Spell and grammar checkers catch typos but miss meaning and tone — don't follow them blindly.

### Peer review

Read as a reader and mark where you got confused. Comment on **content before grammar**, be specific and kind, and let the writer decide the fix.

### Summary

Revise in layers, keep reference and flow clear, cut wordiness, check agreement and punctuation, read aloud, and review ideas before typos.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Trim: *Due to the fact that it was raining, we made the decision to postpone.* → *Because it was raining, we decided to postpone.*

**Example 2.** Fix the vague pronoun: *Tom told David that his report was late.* → name whose report it is.

**Example 3.** Fill in: *The first plan was too costly. ___, we chose a cheaper one.* → **Therefore / So**

**Example 4.** Which layer? a typo = **proofread**; a missing counterargument = **content**; scrambled paragraphs = **structure**.

**Example 5.** Fix: *Their going to the office, but the manager don't no its closed.* → *They're going to the office, but the manager doesn't know it's closed.*

## Exercises

1. Correct order of the revision layers?
2. Shorten *at this point in time.*
3. Fix the comma splice: *I was tired, I went to bed.*
4. Fix: *The team is winning, but they doesn't celebrate.*
5. Why not blindly accept every checker suggestion?
6. In peer review, what comes first?

**Answer key.** (1) content → structure → language → proofread; (2) now; (3) *…tired, so I went to bed* / *…tired; I went…*; (4) *…but it doesn't celebrate* (or *the team are … they don't*); (5) tools miss meaning and tone; (6) content and structure before grammar.
