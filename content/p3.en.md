## Core Notes

### Stating an opinion

Open with an **opinion signal** so people know it's your view, not a fact. Adjust the strength:

| Strength | Frames |
|---|---|
| Neutral | *In my opinion… / I'd say…* |
| Tentative | *It seems to me… / I tend to think…* |
| Strong | *I'm convinced that… / There's no doubt that…* |

Separate fact from opinion, and add a reason: *…because / the reason is…*.

### Agreeing & partial agreement

- **Full:** *Exactly. / That's a good point. / I couldn't agree more.*
- **Partial (agree, then hold back):** *I see your point, but… / True to some extent… / I agree up to a point, however…*

Partial agreement stays polite while leaving room to differ.

### Disagreeing & conceding

- **Polite disagreement:** *I'm not sure I agree. / I see it differently. / With respect, …* (not *You're wrong.*)
- **Concede, then counter:** *That may be true, but… / Even so… / Yes, but on the other hand…*
- **Support with examples:** *For instance… / Take … as an example.*

### Group discussion roles

Discussion is teamwork, not a monologue:

| Role | Language |
|---|---|
| Chair | *Let's start. / What do you think, Mei?* |
| Contributor | *Could I add something? / Building on that…* |
| Summarizer | *So, to sum up…* |

- **Invite others:** *What's your take? / Anything to add?*
- **Take a turn:** *Could I just come in here?*
- **Restate, then respond:** *So what you're saying is… / If I understand you correctly…*
- Don't monopolize — hand the turn back.

### Debate structure

A debate turns on a **motion**; the proposition supports, the opposition opposes.

| Stage | Content |
|---|---|
| Opening | define the motion, state position |
| Arguments | point – reason – example |
| Rebuttal | answer the other side |
| Summary | restate position |

Signpost: *My first argument is… / Furthermore… / To sum up…*.

### Summary

Signal your opinion and give a reason; prefer partial agreement; concede before you counter; invite and restate in groups; argue with point–reason–example.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Soften: *Remote work is better.* → *It seems to me remote work is better, mainly because it saves commuting.*

**Example 2.** Polite disagreement: *You're wrong, cars are bad.* → *I see your point, but cars aren't the whole problem.*

**Example 3.** Concede + counter: *Money brings happiness.* → *That may be true up to a point, but beyond a certain income it makes little difference.*

**Example 4.** Invite a quiet member: *We've heard from Sam — Lena, what's your take?*

**Example 5.** Point–reason–example: *My first argument is that phones distract students. The reason is notifications break focus. For example, one class that banned phones saw better attention.*

## Exercises

1. Give two tentative and one strong opinion frame.
2. Rewrite politely: *No, that's stupid.*
3. Use *That may be true, but…* to answer *English is the only language worth learning.*
4. Write one line to invite someone, one to take your turn.
5. Restate and respond using *So what you're saying is…*.
6. Write point + reason + example on any motion.

**Answer key.** 1: *It seems to me… / I tend to think…*; *I'm convinced that…*. 2: *I see it differently.* 3/4/5/6 vary — check the structure.
