## Core Notes

### Short piece: letters, notices, memos

Practical writing, ~100 words, 10 points — most often a **letter/email**, sometimes a **notice**. Graded on format, task completion, and appropriate tone. Letter in three short paragraphs: **purpose** (why you write), **details** (2–3 points), **closing** (polite sign-off). Address as *Dear …,* and always sign **Li Ming** — never your real name. A notice: centered title, body (what/when/where/who), then the issuing unit and date.

### Long essay: three-paragraph picture/chart

English I usually sets a **picture/cartoon** (20 points, 160–200 words); English II a **chart** (15 points, ~150 words). Both use three paragraphs:

| Paragraph | Picture essay | Chart essay |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | describe the picture | describe data: overall trend + highlights |
| 2 | explain the meaning / causes | analyze the causes |
| 3 | conclusion + suggestion | conclusion / forecast |

Keep paragraph 1 objective; make paragraph 2 the core (reasons + examples); give a clear stance in paragraph 3.

### Intro, body, conclusion templates

Memorize **function-sentence** frames, then fill in content:

- Picture: *As is vividly shown in the picture, …*
- To the meaning: *Simple as it is, the drawing conveys a thought-provoking message: …*
- Reasons: *There are several reasons. To begin with, … Moreover, … A case in point is …*
- Chart: *According to the chart, the number of … rose from … to …, while …*
- Ending: *In light of the above, it is high time that we took steps to … Only in this way can we …*

Short piece: open with *I am writing to …*; close with *I am looking forward to your reply.*

### Advanced patterns and cohesion

Upgrade plain sentences: *I think we should…* → *It is high time that we took action.*; *Many people…* → *An increasing number of people…*. Use emphasis (*It is … that …*), inversion (*Only in this way can we …*), and subject clauses (*What matters most is …*). Link ideas with connectors: **addition** (furthermore), **cause** (as a result), **contrast** (by contrast), **example** (for instance), **summary** (in conclusion).

### Model essays and imitation

Use models as scaffolding: (1) extract each paragraph's function sentences into your own template; (2) reuse the frame on common topics (environment, honesty, phone addiction); (3) write under time — long essay ~30 min, short ~15 min, both within **40–45 minutes**, long essay first (worth more).

### Summary

Short piece: purpose–details–closing, full format, sign **Li Ming**. Long essay: describe → analyze (core) → conclude/suggest. Keep frames ready; picture objective, chart trend-first. Upgrade with emphasis/inversion/subject clauses and connectors. Extract, re-topic, time yourself.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Recommend a novel to your teacher → open: *I am writing to recommend a Chinese novel that I believe you will enjoy.*

**Example 2.** Describe the cartoon → *As is vividly shown in the cartoon, a young man is absorbed in his phone, ignoring an elderly passenger beside him.*

**Example 3.** Visitors up from 200,000 (2018) to 500,000 (2023) → *According to the chart, the number of visitors climbed from 200,000 in 2018 to 500,000 in 2023, a more than twofold increase.*

**Example 4.** Upgrade *I think we should protect the environment.* → *It is high time that we took real action to protect the environment. Only in this way can we leave a greener planet to future generations.*

**Example 5.** Close a phone-addiction essay → *In light of the above, it is high time that we put down our phones. Only through self-discipline can we become masters of technology rather than its slaves.*

## Exercises

1. What name must you sign on the short piece? Your real name?
2. The three paragraph functions of the long essay?
3. What does a chart essay's paragraph 1 give first?
4. Upgrade *Many people use phones too much.*
5. Give three cause-effect connectors.
6. Long or short essay first, and why?

**Answer key.** 1) **Li Ming**; never your real name 2) describe → analyze causes/meaning → conclude + suggest 3) the overall trend, then the highlights 4) e.g. *An increasing number of people have become addicted to their smartphones.* 5) as a result / consequently / therefore 6) long essay first — it is worth more.
