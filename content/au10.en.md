## Transcript

Good afternoon, everyone, and thank you for coming to today's short study session.

I want to share one simple idea that can really improve the way you revise: spread your practice out over time instead of cramming.

Cramming — studying everything the night before an exam — might help you pass, but you forget most of it within a week.

When you study a little every day, your brain has to recall the information again and again, and that repeated effort makes the memory much stronger.

This method is often called spaced practice, and researchers have studied it for over a hundred years.

It also lowers your stress, because you are not trying to learn a whole term's work in one long, tiring night.

For example, if you have thirty new words to learn, review six of them each day for five days rather than all thirty at once.

By the end of the week, those words will feel familiar, and you will remember them long after the test.

So my advice is simple: make a short study plan, and start a few weeks before your exams, not the night before.

Try it with just one subject this month, and I think you will be surprised by the difference.
