## Core Notes

### Email etiquette

A clear email = **good subject + purpose in the first line + a fitting greeting and sign-off.**

- **Subject:** specific and searchable. ✗ *Question* → ✓ *Question about Friday's invoice (No. 3021)*
- **First line states the purpose:** *I'm writing to ask… / Quick question about… / Following up on…*
- **Match greeting and sign-off to formality:**

| Formality | Greeting | Sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| Formal (client, first contact) | Dear Ms. Lee, | Best regards, |
| Neutral (colleague) | Hi Sarah, | Best, / Thanks, |
| Casual (someone you know) | Hey Tom, | Cheers, |

- Use *Could you… / Would you mind…* instead of commands; end with a clear next step.

### How instant messaging differs

- **Shorter, faster:** one point per message; no long paragraphs.
- **Use threads;** @-mention only when you need attention.
- **Don't just say "Hi" and stop** — ask the whole question at once.
- **Emoji in moderation:** a thumbs-up to confirm is fine; go light in formal or bad-news moments.

| Channel | Tone | Emoji | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email | More formal | Few | Records, external, formal requests |
| Chat/IM | Light | Some | Quick teamwork, internal questions |
| Social media | Public, casual | Many | Brand, non-private content |

### Attachments & CC

- **Flag attachments:** *Please find attached… / I've attached…* (and actually attach it before sending).
- **CC** keeps someone informed; they need not reply.
- **BCC** hides recipients from each other.
- **Reply All** only when everyone truly needs it.

### Handy templates

**Business request (fill in line by line):**

- **Subject:** *Request for Q3 sales figures*
- **Greeting:** *Dear Mr. Adams,*
- **Opening:** *I hope you're well. I'm writing to request the Q3 sales figures for the north region.*
- **Body:** *Could you send them by Thursday, 26 June? This will help us finalise the board report.*
- **Close:** *Thank you very much. Best regards, Mia*

**Polite chaser:**

- *Just a gentle nudge on my note below — no rush, but I'd like to keep us on track for Thursday. Thanks so much!*

**Across time zones:** *No rush — whenever it's morning your side is fine.*

### A culture note

- **Direct vs indirect:** US email tends to be direct (*Please send it today.*); over-hedging can read as unclear. Still add *please* or *could*.
- **Over-apologising:** starting every mail with *Sorry to bother you* can sound unsure — try *Thanks for your help with this.*
- **Time zones:** name the zone (*3 pm CET*); don't assume the reader shares your clock.

### Summary

Specific subject, purpose-first opening, formality-matched greeting. Email, chat, and social differ in tone and emoji. Confirm attachments; CC to inform; use Reply All sparingly; keep chasers light and state time zones.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Fix the subject and opener.

> **Answer.** Subject *Question about Friday's invoice (No. 3021)*; opener *I'm writing to check one item on invoice 3021.*

**Example 2.** Soften a command: *Send me the report today.* → **Could you send me the report by end of day? Thanks!**

**Example 3.** Write a polite chaser (no reply for 3 days).

> **Answer.** *Hi Lena, just following up on my request from Monday — no rush, but could you let me know the status when you get a chance? Thanks!*

**Example 4.** Fix: *I attach you the file. I reply all everyone.* → *I've attached the file. I'll reply to everyone only if needed.*

**Example 5.** Cross-time-zone meeting.

> **Answer.** *Would Wednesday at 10 am CET (4 pm your time) work for a quick call? If not, send a couple of slots that suit you.*

## Exercises

1. Name two features of a good subject line.
2. Difference between CC and BCC?
3. Fix: *Please find attach the report.*
4. Soften: *Give me your feedback now.*
5. Write one light, polite chaser line.
6. When scheduling with an overseas colleague, what should you include?

**Answer key.** 1) Specific and searchable. 2) CC recipients are visible to all; BCC recipients are hidden from each other. 3) *Please find **attached** the report.* 4) e.g. *Could you share your feedback when you have a moment?* 5) e.g. *Just a gentle nudge on my last email — thanks!* 6) The time zone (and/or their local time).
