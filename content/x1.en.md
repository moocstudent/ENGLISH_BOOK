## Core Notes

### Two tests at a glance

Both IELTS (Academic) and TOEFL iBT test four skills, but differ in style, scale, and format.

| Feature | IELTS Academic | TOEFL iBT |
|---|---|---|
| Score | 0–9, half bands | 0–120, four sections of 0–30 |
| Skills | Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking | Reading, Listening, Speaking, Writing |
| Speaking | Face-to-face with an examiner | Recorded into a microphone |
| Writing | Chart report + essay | Integrated task + academic discussion |
| Accent | Mainly British | Mainly American |

- Prefer talking to a person → IELTS; comfortable typing and recording → TOEFL.
- Formats and timings are updated periodically — check the official guide before you book.

### Reading & listening question map

- **IELTS reading:** True/False/Not Given, Yes/No/Not Given, matching headings, summary completion, MCQ.
- **TOEFL reading:** factual and negative factual, inference, vocabulary, sentence simplification, insert text, prose summary (worth 2).
- **Time traps:** *Not Given* is not *False* — decide only from the text. T/F/NG asks about facts; Y/N/NG asks about the writer's views.

### Reading the writing rubric

| Band area | What it means |
|---|---|
| Task response | On topic, all parts covered, length met |
| Coherence & cohesion | Logic, linkers, clear reference |
| Lexical resource | Accurate, varied, natural collocation |
| Grammar | Range of structures, accuracy |

**Essay skeleton:** intro (paraphrase + position) → body 1 (point, explain, example) → body 2 (reason or counter) → conclusion (restate + outlook). Structure beats rare words.

### Speaking criteria

- **IELTS:** fluency & coherence, lexical resource, grammar, pronunciation — three parts, including a 1–2 minute talk.
- **TOEFL:** delivery, language use, topic development — one independent task plus three integrated tasks.
- Organise with signposts: *First… / For example… / That's why…*; take notes before integrated tasks.

### An 8-week plan

Assume about 10 hours a week, finishing with real past papers.

| Weeks | Focus | Daily (~1 hr) | Weekend |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Diagnose, learn task types | 1 reading + 1 listening | 1 diagnostic set |
| 3–4 | Speed up reading/listening | 1 timed set | Review errors |
| 5–6 | Writing templates | 1 paragraph + collocations | Full essay + feedback |
| 7 | Speaking sprint | Record 2 answers | 1 mock speaking |
| 8 | Full mocks | Fix weak areas | 1 timed full mock |

Calibrate with **real past papers**; keep an "error-cause list" after each mock.

### Summary

Compare structure, then choose. *Not Given* is not *False*, and view questions differ from fact questions. Writing is judged on four areas — structure before rare words; finish prep with timed mocks on real papers.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Essay intro template (topic: universities should teach job skills, not academic knowledge).

> **Answer.** *Many argue that universities ought to prioritise practical skills over pure academic study. While such training matters, I believe a strong academic foundation remains essential.*

**Example 2.** Make it more academic: *A lot of people think it's a really big problem.* → **Many people regard it as a serious problem.**

**Example 3.** Speaking (describe a skill you want to learn).

> **Answer.** *One skill I'd love to master is public speaking, mainly because it would boost my confidence at work. For example, I often present to clients and still get nervous.*

**Example 4.** Fix: *There are many reason why people prefers online learning.* → *…many **reasons** why people **prefer**…*

**Example 5.** Body paragraph (remote work).

> **Answer.** *One clear benefit of remote work is flexibility. Because employees save commuting time, they can focus on results rather than hours. This, in turn, helps many parents balance childcare with deadlines.*

## Exercises

1. Info is not mentioned in the passage — True, False, or Not Given?
2. Biggest difference between IELTS and TOEFL speaking?
3. Fix: *He gave three reason for his decision.*
4. Make academic: *Lots of kids are bad at saving money.*
5. Write one position sentence with *I believe…* or *In my view…*.
6. Name the four writing rubric areas.

**Answer key.** 1) Not Given. 2) IELTS is face-to-face; TOEFL is recorded. 3) three **reasons**. 4) e.g. *Many young people struggle to save money.* 5) e.g. *In my view, schools should teach financial skills.* 6) task response, coherence & cohesion, lexical resource, grammar.
