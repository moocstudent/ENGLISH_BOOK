## Core Notes

### IPA basics

English spelling ≠ sound (*through / though / tough*). IPA gives **one symbol per sound**; dictionaries show it in /.../. Read it instead of guessing from letters.

- **Vowels:** short /ɪ e æ ʌ ɒ ʊ ə/ vs long /iː ɑː ɔː uː ɜː/.
- **Length mark ː:** holds the vowel (/iː/ *sheep* longer than /ɪ/ *ship*).
- **Stress mark ˈ:** before the stressed syllable — *about* /əˈbaʊt/.

| Symbol | Example |
|---|---|
| /iː/ | see, tea |
| /ɪ/ | sit, big |
| /æ/ | cat, bad |
| /ə/ | about, teacher |
| /θ/ | think, three |

### Vowel contrasts

Train your ear with **minimal pairs** (words differing by one sound):

| Contrast | Pair |
|---|---|
| /iː/ vs /ɪ/ | sheep / ship |
| /æ/ vs /e/ | bad / bed |
| /ʌ/ vs /ɑː/ | cut / cart |

Schwa /ə/ is the most common sound and appears only in **unstressed** syllables: *banana* /bəˈnɑːnə/.

### Consonants: th / v / r

- **/θ/ and /ð/:** tongue tip at the teeth. Voiceless *think, three*; voiced *this, mother*. Don't say *sink* for *think*.
- **/v/:** teeth on lip, voiced — *vest* not *west*, *very* not *ferry*.
- **/r/:** no trill, no tap; tongue pulls back — *red, right*. Keep *rice* ≠ *lice*.
- Don't add a vowel after final consonants (*book*, not "book-uh").

### Stress changes word class

Same spelling, different stress = noun vs verb:

| Word | Noun | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| record | ˈrecord | reˈcord |
| present | ˈpresent | preˈsent |
| object | ˈobject | obˈject |

Rule of thumb: two-syllable nouns/adjectives stress the first syllable, verbs the second. Suffixes shift stress: *ˈphotograph → phoˈtography → photoˈgraphic*.

### Rhythm & chunking

- English is **stress-timed**: content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs) are stressed; function words reduce.
- **Weak forms:** *to* /tə/, *and* /ən/, *of* /əv/, *can* /kən/.
- **Chunking:** pause at meaning boundaries — *When I finish work / I'll call you.*
- **Linking:** *an apple* → "a-napple".
- Record yourself, compare with a dictionary, fix the stress and vowels.

### Summary

Read IPA instead of guessing; drill vowels with minimal pairs; master th/v/r; put stress in the right place; keep content words strong and function words weak.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** /iː/ vs /ɪ/: *sheep* vs ___ → **ship** (also seat/sit, feel/fill).

**Example 2.** *They will record a song* — ˈrecord or reˈcord? → **reˈcord** (verb).

**Example 3.** Smooth it: *I want to go home.* → *I wanna go home* (*to* → /tə/, content words strong).

**Example 4.** Mind the *th*: *Is this the third one?* — this/the = /ð/, third = /θ/.

**Example 5.** Mark the stress: *Sales GREW by twenty PERCENT this QUARTER.*
