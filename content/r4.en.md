## Core Notes

### Inverted pyramid

News reports put the **most important facts first**, the least important last.

The **lead** (first paragraph) answers the key **5W1H**: Who, What, When, Where, Why, How.

| Position | Content | Cuttable? |
|---|---|---|
| Lead | core facts | no |
| Early body | key detail, background | keep |
| End | minor detail | yes |

Short on time? Read the **lead plus the first two paragraphs**. Features do the opposite.

### Features and profiles

A **feature** values story and detail over speed.

- Often an **anecdotal / scene-setting lead**: a small story first.
- The **nut graph** (what it is really about) comes around paragraph 3–5.
- A **profile** paints a person through anecdotes and quotes.

Don't expect the conclusion in line one — read to the nut graph.

### Columns and opinion

A **column / editorial** states a **view**, not neutral news.

- The author takes a **stance**; expect evaluative words, rhetorical questions, analogy.
- Shape: claim → support → **concession** → restatement.
- Concession signals: *Of course…, Admittedly…, To be fair…* then *but / yet*.

Find the claim (start or end), then see how it is supported.

### Fake news and source-checking

- **Source:** a named reporter and real outlet, or an anonymous forward?
- **Evidence:** specific origin, data, more than one side?
- **Date:** old news recycled?
- **Language:** ALL CAPS, "SHOCKING" → clickbait.
- **Cross-check:** do several trusted outlets report it?

Mind the label: **News vs Opinion vs Analysis vs Sponsored**.

### A reading plan

| Stage | Material | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Start | graded / learner news | one short piece daily |
| Build | mainstream short reports | read the lead, note 3 words |
| Stretch | long features, columns | one close read weekly |

A fixed time, a vocabulary notebook (word plus sentence), and a **one-sentence summary** after each piece keep the habit alive.

### Summary

Reports front-load facts; features hide the point in the nut graph; columns argue; always check source, evidence, date, and language.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Lead: *A fire in an empty warehouse near the harbour was brought under control early Tuesday, with no injuries reported.* → key = **no injuries; fire controlled**.

**Example 2.** *"It was still dark when Maria unlocked her bakery…"* → **feature** (scene-setting), not a news report.

**Example 3.** *Admittedly, the rule is well-intentioned. But good intentions rarely pay the bills.* → concession; author **disagrees**.

**Example 4.** *"You Won't BELIEVE What This City Did — Doctors Are Furious!"* → **clickbait**; verify the source.

**Example 5.** *"Sources who declined to be named said the deal might collapse."* → anonymous plus *might* → **unverified**, cross-check.

## Exercises

1. In the inverted pyramid, what do you read first when short on time?
2. Where is a feature's nut graph, usually?
3. Which reads like a column? A) The bill passed with a clear majority. B) This reckless bill will haunt us for years.
4. List three checks for whether news is trustworthy.
5. *"To be fair, the plan has merits, yet…"* — support or oppose?

**Answer key.** 1) the lead plus the first paragraphs. 2) around paragraph 3–5, not the first. 3) B. 4) source, evidence, date, cross-check (any three). 5) oppose.
