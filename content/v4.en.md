## Core Notes

### Idioms vs literal phrases

An **idiom** has a meaning you cannot get from the words: **break the ice** (start a conversation), **a piece of cake** (very easy), **under the weather** (unwell).

| Phrase | Literal? | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| open the door | yes | open the door |
| break the ice | no | ease tension |
| hit the books | no | study hard |

Learn idioms as whole chunks — you can't swap a word (*a slice of cake* is wrong).

### Metaphor and origin

Most idioms come from a **metaphor**, and the source helps you remember:

- **spill the beans** = reveal a secret
- **the ball is in your court** = it's your decision (from tennis)
- **the tip of the iceberg** = a small visible part of a big problem

Group idioms by image (sport, food, weather, sailing).

### Business and everyday idioms

Everyday: **hang out**, **grab a bite**, **call it a day**, **get the hang of it**.

| Business idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| touch base | check in briefly |
| think outside the box | be creative |
| in the loop | kept informed |
| back to the drawing board | start over |

Idioms are mostly spoken or semi-formal — avoid them in formal essays.

### Fixed sentence frames

- **It's no use + V-ing:** *It's no use crying over spilt milk.*
- **Would you mind + V-ing?**
- **The sooner …, the better.**
- **not only … but also …**

**Binomials** have a fixed order: *back and forth, sooner or later, safe and sound* — never reversed.

### Cross-cultural pitfalls

Idioms carry culture; don't translate word-for-word. Chinese 拍马屁 is **butter someone up** / **suck up to**, not a literal horse phrase. Rules: understand before using, don't overuse, match your audience, and watch for dated idioms.

### Summary

Keep idioms whole, learn them by image, match register, and translate the meaning — not the words.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Start with a joke to ___. → **break the ice**

**Example 2.** Fix: *a slice of cake* → **a piece of cake**

**Example 3.** Translate: 冰山一角 → **the tip of the iceberg**

**Example 4.** Meanings: touch base / call it a day / in the loop → check in / stop work / informed

**Example 5.** Use a business idiom: *The design failed, so we went back to the drawing board.*

## Exercises

1. Meaning of *under the weather* and *a piece of cake*.
2. The ball is in your ___ (court / hand).
3. Fix: *It's no use to cry over spilt milk.*
4. Complete: safe and ___; sooner or ___.
5. Register: is *touch base* better for an essay or a work email?
6. English idiom for 拍马屁?

**Answer key.** 1) unwell; very easy  2) court  3) crying (no use + V-ing)  4) sound; later  5) work email  6) butter someone up / suck up to.
