## Core Notes

### Format and scoring

A 240–280 word passage with **20 blanks**, four options each. Same in English I and II: **10 points total, 0.5 each**. It tests **discourse**, not isolated vocabulary — usually you know all four words; the skill is picking the one that fits.

- ~70% of blanks test **logic and word sense**; ~30% test **collocation, prepositions, connectors**.
- Low value, high time cost — finish in about **15 minutes** and save time for reading.

### First sentence sets the tone

The opening sentence is almost never blanked. Read the whole passage once (~40 seconds) before choosing: get the **topic** and the author's **stance (positive or negative)**, then work blank by blank. Answers often depend on **later** text, so do the easy ones first and re-read to check.

### Logic: cause, contrast, addition

Blanks often test the link between sentences. Signal words point to the answer:

| Relation | Signals | Hint |
|---|---|---|
| Contrast | but, however, yet, while, instead | sides are opposite |
| Cause–effect | because, therefore, thus, so, as a result | find cause or result |
| Addition | and, also, moreover, besides | same direction |
| Example | for example, that is, such as | sense shown by the example |
| Concession | although, though, despite | clauses conflict |

*The plan looked perfect on paper; ___, it failed in practice.* → **however** (contrast), not *therefore*.

### Word sense and collocation

Two elimination tools: **fixed collocations** (only one word fits — *in the absence of*, *account for*, *throw oneself into*, *be characterized by*) and **sense** (tell near-synonyms apart by tone, degree, object). Note *affect* (verb) vs *effect* (noun).

### Drill on a real passage (go to k5)

A full Kaoyan-style cloze is already worked in **chapter k5, "A Future Without Work"** — 20 blanks, parallel translation, every key explained. Do it there; this chapter does not repeat that passage. After each wrong answer, ask: was it logic, collocation, or sense? which signal fixed it? why did the distractor fool me?

### Summary

20 blanks, 10 points, ~15 min. Read first for tone; contrast reverses, cause follows; lock with collocation; drill in k5.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** *New tools save time, ___ they also create problems.* → **but** (benefit vs drawback: contrast).

**Example 2.** *The road was closed. ___, we took a detour.* → **Therefore** (cause → result).

**Example 3.** *She succeeded ___ the absence of support.* → **in** (*in the absence of*, fixed phrase).

**Example 4.** *The medicine had little ___ on his headache.* → **effect** (after *little* a noun; *affect* is a verb).

**Example 5.** Timing 20 blanks in 15 min → skim ~1.5, blanks ~10 (skip hard ones), re-check ~3. Never spend over ~40s on one blank.

## Exercises

1. *Sales rose in spring; ___, they fell in summer.* (however / therefore)
2. *He was tired, ___ he kept working.* (so / but)
3. *Her success came ___ the expense of her health.* (preposition)
4. *This factor may ___ the decision.* (affect / effect)
5. *The report tries to ___ for the change.* (account / count)
6. Max seconds on one blank before guessing?

**Answer key.** 1) however 2) but 3) at 4) affect 5) account 6) ~40s, then guess and mark it.
