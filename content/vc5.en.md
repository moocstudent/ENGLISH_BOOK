## Word List

| Word / phrase | POS | IPA | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| affect / effect | v / n | /əˈfekt/, /ɪˈfekt/ | affect = to influence (verb); effect = a result (noun) | The weather can affect your mood, and that effect is real. |
| principle / principal | n / adj,n | /ˈprɪnsəpl/ | principle = a rule or belief; principal = main, or the head of a school | On principle, the school principal treats every student fairly. |
| advice / advise | n / v | /ədˈvaɪs/, /ədˈvaɪz/ | advice = a suggestion (noun); advise = to give advice (verb) | She advised me to rest, and it was good advice. |
| rise / raise | vi / vt | /raɪz/, /reɪz/ | rise = go up by itself (no object); raise = lift something (needs an object) | Prices rise every year, and shops raise their prices in summer. |
| lie / lay | vi / vt | /laɪ/, /leɪ/ | lie = to recline (no object); lay = to put something down (needs an object) | I lie on the sofa and lay a blanket over my legs. |
| sensible / sensitive | adj | /ˈsensəbl/, /ˈsensətɪv/ | sensible = practical and wise; sensitive = easily affected or caring about feelings | It was sensible to bring an umbrella, but don't be so sensitive about the joke. |
| historic / historical | adj | /hɪˈstɒrɪk/, /hɪˈstɒrɪkl/ | historic = important in history; historical = relating to the past | The signing was a historic event, based on real historical records. |
| economic / economical | adj | /ˌiːkəˈnɒmɪk/, /ˌiːkəˈnɒmɪkl/ | economic = about the economy; economical = saving money or resources | The country's economic growth is strong, and this small car is very economical. |

## Notes

- **This deck**: 18 commonly confused words, each with part of speech, IPA, meanings and an example that pulls the pair apart.
- **Memory tip**: anchor each pair to one clear difference — *advice* is the noun, *advise* is the verb; you *borrow* from someone but *lend* to someone.
