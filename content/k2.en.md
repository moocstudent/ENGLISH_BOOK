## Core Notes

### Five Part-A question types

Part A: **4 passages, 20 questions, 2 points each = 40 points** — the highest-value section. Read the **question first** (not the options), locate in the text, and answer from **evidence**. Questions usually follow the passage order.

| Type | Cue words | Move |
|---|---|---|
| Main idea | mainly about, best title, purpose | first/last paragraphs; pick the full, not partial |
| Detail | according to, mentions | locate and compare to the text |
| Inference | infer, imply, suggest | one small step from the text |
| Attitude | attitude, tone | find evaluative words |
| Vocabulary/reference | the word X means, it refers to | read back into context |

### Distractor patterns

The right option is usually a **paraphrase** of the text; word-for-word copies are often traps.

| Trap | Looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Not stated | absent from the text | no source → out |
| Swapped concept | changed subject/time/object | check word by word |
| Over-generalization | part passed off as whole | main idea must be full |
| Too extreme | all/only/must/never | extremes usually wrong |
| Reversed | positive made negative | watch not/no/hardly |
| True but unsupported | correct yet not in text | needs a source |

### Part B: gapped text (7 choose 5)

Five gaps, **seven options, two extra**, 10 points. Tests cohesion. Use clues around the gap: **pronouns** (this, these, such), **word repetition**, **connectors** starting the option (However, Also, For example), and **articles** (a = first mention, the = already mentioned). Do the clearest gaps first; re-read to check; don't force the two distractors in.

### Part B: ordering and headings

**Ordering:** find the **first paragraph** (opens the topic, no back-reference); follow the chain of pronouns, *the* + noun, time words, connectors; fix adjacent pairs first. **Headings:** read each paragraph's **first and last sentence**, then match; beware headings that cover only one detail.

### Close reading and error analysis

After answering: clear every unknown word and long sentence (see k3); **mark the evidence sentence** for each question; diagnose each miss — locating, understanding, or a distractor? Pace: ~16–18 min per Part-A passage, ~18–20 min for Part B.

### Summary

Main idea = full not partial; detail = locate; inference = one step; attitude = evaluative words. Six distractor traps; right answers paraphrase the text. Gapped text runs on pronouns and connectors; ordering on the first paragraph and reference chains.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Passage: remote work raises output but weakens team spirit. *Mainly about?* → **the mixed effects of remote work** — main idea must be full; "how to work from home" is partial.

**Example 2.** *…but only large ones reported gains.* Who reported gains? → **large firms**. Distractors say *all firms* (over-general) or change the year (swap).

**Example 3.** *The so-called breakthrough rests on shaky evidence.* Attitude? → **doubtful/critical** — *so-called* and *shaky* are negative cues.

**Example 4.** Gap followed by *This shift, however, came at a price.* → choose a sentence naming a **shift**, referred to by *This* and set against by *however*.

**Example 5.** First paragraph: the one that **opens the topic** with no back-reference (*In 2010, the city launched…*), not one starting *For this reason…*.

## Exercises

1. Which type must be "full, not partial"?
2. What do all/only/never in an option often signal?
3. Are right answers usually copies or paraphrases?
4. A gap is followed by *These problems…* — what must the answer contain?
5. Typical mark of a first paragraph in ordering?
6. Which two sentences matter most for headings?

**Answer key.** 1) main idea 2) a too-extreme distractor 3) paraphrases 4) a plural "problems" it can refer back to, placed before it 5) opens the topic, no back-reference 6) first and last.
