## Passage

*— Adapted from A Christmas Carol, Charles Dickens (1843). A public-domain work.*

Oh! but he was a tight-fisted old man, this Ebenezer Scrooge — a hard, sharp, grasping man who loved money and little else. Cold seemed to live inside him. It stiffened his old face, made his eyes red and his thin lips blue, and put a frosty edge on his scratchy voice. He was, as people said, "solitary as an oyster."

The cold within him went with him everywhere. He chilled his office even in summer, and no fire could warm him at Christmas. Heat and cold from the outside meant nothing to him; no wind was harsher than he was, no falling snow more set on its purpose.

Nobody ever stopped him in the street to greet him kindly, to ask how he was or when he might visit. No beggar dared to ask him for a coin; no child asked him the time; not once in his whole life did a stranger ask him the way. Even the dogs that led blind men seemed to know him, and would pull their owners into doorways to keep out of his sight.

But did Scrooge mind? Not at all. It was exactly what he liked. To push through the busy crowds of life while warning every warm feeling to keep its distance — that suited him perfectly. He asked nothing of other people, and he wished them to ask nothing of him.

## Notes

- **Source / author:** *A Christmas Carol* (1843) is a famous short novel by the English writer Charles Dickens, telling how the miser Scrooge is changed by ghosts on Christmas Eve. This passage is an adaptation of the opening portrait of Scrooge, not the original text.
- **Main idea:** A chain of "cold" images paints Scrooge as a cold, greedy man who keeps everyone at a distance, preparing the reader for his later change.
- **Structure / craft:** The passage turns on one central metaphor — inner cold — and adds a list of details (no greetings, no beggars, no children, the blind men's dogs) that pile up to stress his loneliness.
- **Tone:** Exaggerated and ironic, both harsh and quietly humorous.

## Reading Comprehension

1. Which statement best sums up how Scrooge is presented in the passage?
- A) A kind and generous old man
- B) A poor beggar who lives in the street
- C) A cheerful friend to the children of the town
- D) A cold, greedy man who keeps everyone away

2. According to the passage, how does the cold affect Scrooge?
- A) It makes him look young and healthy.
- B) It makes him kinder to strangers.
- C) It forces him to light a huge fire in summer.
- D) It reddens his eyes and turns his thin lips blue.

3. What does the detail about the blind men's dogs suggest about Scrooge?
- A) He is loved by animals.
- B) He often stops to feed stray dogs.
- C) Even animals avoid him because he is so unfriendly.
- D) He is famous and well liked in the town.

4. In "a tight-fisted old man," the phrase *tight-fisted* most nearly means
- A) very strong
- B) unwilling to spend money; mean
- C) quick to become angry
- D) hard-working and busy

5. How does the writer mainly build this picture of Scrooge?
- A) By repeating images of cold and listing the people who avoid him
- B) By describing his happy childhood in detail
- C) By quoting the kind words he says to his neighbours
- D) By explaining how much money he gives to the poor

**Answer Key**

1) **D** — Main idea. The passage stresses again and again that he is "tight-fisted," "grasping," and "loved money and little else," and that everyone avoids him. B fits best; there is no sign of generosity, friendship or begging.
2) **D** — Detail. The text says the inner cold "made his eyes red and his thin lips blue." Outside heat and cold "meant nothing to him," so C and D are wrong.
3) **C** — Inference. Even the dogs guiding blind men "pull their owners into doorways to keep out of his sight," suggesting he is so unfriendly that even animals keep away.
4) **B** — Vocabulary in context. *Tight-fisted* pictures a closed fist that will not let money go; it means mean or unwilling to spend, matching "grasping" and "loved money."
5) **A** — Craft. The writer repeats cold images (cold, frosty, chilled) and lists people who avoid him — no greetings, beggars, children, or even dogs. He does not describe a childhood, kind words, or charity.
