## Core Notes

### Independent vs subordinate clauses

An **independent clause** stands alone (*I like coffee*). A **subordinate clause** has a subject and verb but needs a main clause (*…because I like coffee*). A **complex sentence** = main clause + one or more subordinate clauses.

| Clause | Job | Typical link words |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | subject / object | that, whether, if, what, who |
| Adjective (relative) | describes a noun | who, which, that, whose, where |
| Adverb | modifies verb / sentence | because, when, although, if |

### Relative (adjective) clauses

**Pronouns:** who (people), whom (formal object), whose (possession), which (things), that (defining only). **Adverbs:** where, when, why.

- **Defining** — essential, no commas, *that* allowed: *The man **who** called is my boss.*
- **Non-defining** — extra info, commas, no *that*, no omission: *My boss, **who** is Canadian, called.*

Drop the pronoun only when it is the **object**: *the book (that) I bought*; never when it is the subject.

### Noun clauses

Used as subject or object: *That he lied surprised us* / *I think (that) he lied*. Use **whether / if** for yes-no ideas. Noun clauses take **statement word order**.

- ✗ I don't know where is he. → ✓ …where he is.

### Adverb clauses

Time (when, while, before, until), reason (because, since), concession (although, though), condition (if, unless). In time and condition clauses use the **present** for the future: *When I **arrive**, I'll call.*

### Punctuation and position

- Adverb clause first → comma: *Although it rained, we walked.*
- Adverb clause last → usually no comma.
- Non-defining relative → commas around it. Noun clause → no comma.

### Pitfalls

- **Comma splice:** ✗ It was late, I went home. → add *so* or use a semicolon.
- **although … but** or **because … so** together — keep only one.
- ✗ what is this (inside a noun clause) → what **this** is.

### Summary

Main clause + subordinate clause; three clause types; defining vs non-defining commas; statement order in noun clauses; never double a conjunction.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** This is the café ___ we met. → **where**

**Example 2.** Fix: *The house which I grew up in it was small.* → drop **it**.

**Example 3.** Although he was tired, he ___ the report. → **finished** (no *but*).

**Example 4.** I don't remember ___ (where did I put it / where I put it). → **where I put it**

**Example 5.** Combine: *The film won an award. I watched it last night.* → **The film that I watched last night won an award.**

## Exercises

1. The woman ___ car was stolen called the police. → **whose**
2. Fix: *Because it was raining, so we stayed in.* → drop **so**.
3. Tell me ___ (what do you want / what you want). → **what you want**
4. Join with a non-defining clause: *My brother lives in Tokyo. He is a chef.* → **My brother, who is a chef, lives in Tokyo.**
5. Fix the comma splice: *The shop was closed, we went home.* → **…closed, so we went home.**
6. Translate: 我不确定她是否会同意。 → **I'm not sure whether she will agree.**

**Answer key.** 1 whose · 2 drop *so* · 3 what you want · 4 …who is a chef… · 5 add *so* / semicolon · 6 whether she will agree.
