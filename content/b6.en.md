## Passage

*— Adapted from Treasure Island, Robert Louis Stevenson (1883). A public-domain work.*

I still remember the old seaman as clearly as if he had come only yesterday. He arrived slowly at the door of the Admiral Benbow, a large, brown man with a heavy step, his blue coat worn and stained. A long scar crossed one cheek, a dirty white line against his weathered skin, and a wooden sea-chest rolled behind him on a hand-barrow. As he came, he sang an old sailor's song in a high, cracked voice: "Yo-ho-ho, and a bottle of rum!"

He struck the door with his stick, and when my father appeared, the stranger called roughly for a glass of rum. He drank it slowly, like a man who knew his liquor well, and all the while his eyes wandered over the cliffs and up to the signboard above our door.

The old sailor seemed to like what he saw. He told my father that the quiet little bay and the comfortable inn suited him well, and, on hearing that few guests ever came, he decided at once to stay. From his pocket he counted out silver coins, enough to pay for several weeks.

But before he settled by the fire, he drew me aside. Pressing a silver fourpenny into my hand, he made me promise to watch, day and night, for a one-legged sailor, and to warn him the very moment such a man appeared. Something in his low voice made me shiver.

## Vocabulary

| Word / Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| weathered | worn and marked by sun, wind and weather |
| scar | a mark left on the skin by an old wound |
| sea-chest | a wooden box in which a sailor keeps his belongings |
| hand-barrow | a small cart pushed or carried by hand |
| roughly | in a rough, harsh manner |
| liquor | strong alcoholic drink |
| wander (of eyes) | to move slowly here and there |
| signboard | a board showing the name of an inn or shop |
| company | visitors; people who come and go |
| draw someone aside | to lead someone to one side, away from others |
| fourpenny | a coin worth four pennies |
| shiver | to shake slightly, from cold or fear |

## Notes

- **Source / author:** *Treasure Island* (1883) is the pirate-adventure classic of the Scottish writer Robert Louis Stevenson, told in the first person by the boy Jim Hawkins. This passage is an adaptation of the opening, when the old seaman first arrives at the inn — not the original text.
- **Main idea:** A mysterious old sailor, with a scar, a sea-chest and a pirate song, takes a room at the quiet inn and warns the boy to watch for a one-legged sailor, setting up the suspense and the treasure-hunt to come.
- **Structure / craft:** A first-person memory ("I still remember..."). The writer first paints the man's looks (scar, worn coat, sea-chest), then his actions (singing, calling for rum, paying, the warning), building suspense step by step; the closing shiver hints that something is wrong.
- **Tone:** Suspenseful and tense, with a touch of adventure; the calm telling keeps hinting that the stranger hides a dangerous past.

## Reading Comprehension

1. Which words best describe the feeling the old seaman brings to the inn?
- A) Cheerful and safe
- B) Mysterious and slightly threatening
- C) Sad and hopeless
- D) Funny and light-hearted

2. What did the old seaman ask the boy to do?
- A) To carry his sea-chest upstairs
- B) To bring him a map of the coast
- C) To watch for a one-legged sailor and warn him
- D) To keep his rum a secret from Father

3. What does the seaman's warning about a one-legged sailor suggest?
- A) He is looking forward to meeting an old friend.
- B) He fears someone from his past and may be in danger.
- C) He wants to hire more sailors for a voyage.
- D) He is simply making a joke with the boy.

4. In "his eyes wandered over the cliffs," the word *wandered* most nearly means
- A) closed tightly
- B) moved slowly here and there
- C) filled with tears
- D) stared angrily at one spot

5. Why does the writer end with the boy feeling a shiver at the man's low voice?
- A) To show that the room was very cold
- B) To hint that danger and secrets surround the stranger
- C) To prove that the boy was ill
- D) To explain that the man was about to sing again

**Answer Key**

1) **B** — Main idea / mood. The scar, the pirate song, the rough manner, the warning about a one-legged sailor and the closing shiver all build a mysterious, faintly threatening atmosphere — not cheerful (A), hopeless (C) or comic (D).
2) **C** — Detail. The seaman gives the boy a silver fourpenny and asks him to watch for, and warn him about, a one-legged sailor.
3) **B** — Inference. Asking the boy to watch day and night for a one-legged sailor suggests the man fears someone from his past and may be in danger, not meeting a friend (A), hiring sailors (C) or joking (D).
4) **B** — Vocabulary in context. "His eyes wandered over the cliffs" means they moved slowly here and there over the cliffs, so *wandered* means moved slowly here and there.
5) **B** — Craft / tone. Ending with the boy's shiver at the man's low voice hints that danger and secrets surround the stranger, rather than showing cold weather (A) or illness (C).
