## Core Notes

### Guessing words from context

Don't stop for every unknown word. Use clues, then check.

- **Definition / apposition:** dashes or *that is*, *in other words* explain. *A polyglot — someone who speaks many languages — reads fast.*
- **Contrast:** *but, unlike, whereas* signal an opposite. *Unlike his frugal brother, Tom is a spendthrift.* → a big spender.
- **Examples:** *such as*, *for example* let you work backwards.
- **Word parts:** *irreversible* = ir- (not) + revers + -ible → cannot be undone.

Then **plug your guess back in** and see if it fits.

### Attitude and evaluative language

The writer's stance hides in **adjectives, adverbs, and verb choice**, not in plain facts.

| Signal | Attitude |
|---|---|
| remarkable, promising, wisely | positive |
| so-called, allegedly, claims | doubtful |
| troubling, reckless, sadly | critical |
| may, seems, perhaps | cautious |

Neutral reporting uses *said*; loaded reporting uses *admitted, boasted, warned*.

### Common inference questions

- **imply / suggest** — not stated, but supported by the text.
- **would agree** — matches the author's stance.
- **purpose** — why the writer says or gives this.

Rule: inference = **text + one small step**. Jump too far and it's a wrong option.

### Register: formal / neutral / critical

| Tone | Marks | Genre |
|---|---|---|
| formal | full sentences, passive | academic |
| neutral | balanced statement | news report |
| critical / ironic | reversal, exaggeration | column |

**Irony:** literally positive, really negative. Spot it through **contrast with context**: if the passage lists problems but the last line says "a great success," it is ironic.

### Close-reading an opinion piece

> The city has finally opened its long-promised bicycle lane. It runs for a couple of hundred metres before ending, without warning, at a busy roundabout. Planners call this "a first step." Cyclists, forced back into the traffic where the lane stops, may wonder when the second step will arrive. Still, we are told, the ribbon-cutting was a great success.

- **Facts:** a short lane that stops at a roundabout.
- **Loaded words:** *finally*, *without warning*, *a great success* (ironic).
- **Implication:** the author mocks an unfinished, for-show project.

### Summary

Guess words from clues then verify; attitude lives in evaluative words; inference stays close to the text; irony shows through contrast.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** *The road was so treacherous — icy, steep, full of blind curves — that we crawled.* → treacherous ≈ **dangerous**.

**Example 2.** *She read the letter twice, put on her coat, and left without finishing her tea.* → infer **bad / urgent news**.

**Example 3.** *The committee's "brilliant" plan will, no doubt, solve everything — just like its last three.* → attitude: **sarcastic**.

**Example 4.** Fact vs opinion: *The bridge opened in 2019* (fact) vs *The bridge is an ugly waste* (opinion).

**Example 5.** *"Of course the software is perfectly easy to use," she said, staring at the fourth error message.* → **irony**: it is hard to use.

## Exercises

1. Guess: *...made patients* drowsy*, so they should not drive.* → ?
2. Tone of *"So-called experts again failed to predict the obvious."*
3. Which is opinion? A) Water boils at 100°C. B) This is the best café in town.
4. *He checked his watch three times in a ten-minute meeting.* → infer his mood.
5. Irony? *Great — another meeting that could have been an email.*

**Answer key.** 1) sleepy (contrast with *drive*). 2) critical. 3) B. 4) impatient. 5) No — ironic, means annoyed.
