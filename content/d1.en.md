## Passage

*— Adapted from The Gettysburg Address, Abraham Lincoln (1863). A public-domain work.*

About eighty-seven years ago, our founders built a new nation on this continent. They based it on a single belief: that everyone is born free and that all people are equal. Today a great civil war is testing whether a nation founded on such a belief can survive, or whether it must break apart. We have gathered on a famous battlefield of that war.

We have come to set aside part of this field as a final resting place for the men who gave their lives so that the nation might live on. It is entirely right that we should do this. And yet, on a deeper level, our words cannot truly bless this ground or make it holy. The brave soldiers who fought here, the living and the dead alike, have already made it sacred far more than we ever could. People will scarcely remember what we say today, but they can never forget what these men did here.

And so the real work now belongs to us, the living. We must dedicate ourselves to the great task that still remains before us, and draw from these honoured dead a deeper devotion to the cause for which they gave everything they had. Let us firmly resolve that their deaths were not in vain, that this nation shall win a fresh birth of freedom, and that a "government of the people, by the people, for the people" shall never disappear from the earth.

## Vocabulary

| Word / Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| founders | the people who first created a country or thing |
| continent | one of the Earth's large land areas |
| civil war | a war between groups inside one country |
| survive | to continue to exist or stay alive |
| break apart | to split into pieces; to fail and not last |
| battlefield | the ground where a battle is fought |
| resting place | a place where the dead are buried |
| bless / make holy | to make something sacred through words or rites |
| sacred | holy; deserving deep respect |
| dedicate (oneself) to | to give oneself fully to a purpose |
| devotion | deep loyalty and commitment |
| in vain | without success; for nothing |

## Notes

- **Source / author:** *The Gettysburg Address* (1863) is the short speech — only about 272 words — that President Abraham Lincoln gave at the dedication of the national cemetery at Gettysburg. This passage is a simplified adaptation for learners, not the original wording.
- **Main idea:** The nation was founded on freedom and equality; the civil war tests whether such a nation can survive; the living must finish the dead soldiers' unfinished work so that a government "of the people, by the people, for the people" endures.
- **Structure / craft:** It moves step by step — founding, testing, dedicating the ground, then turning to the duty of the living — using contrast (words vs. deeds, the living vs. the dead) and the famous parallel phrase "of the people, by the people, for the people."
- **Tone:** Solemn and dignified, yet inspiring; it rises from mourning to a call for action.

## Reading Comprehension

1. What is the main purpose of the speaker's closing words?
- A) To describe the details of the battle
- B) To thank the government for building the cemetery
- C) To call the living to continue the soldiers' unfinished work
- D) To warn other nations against war

2. According to the passage, on what belief was the nation founded?
- A) That war brings lasting peace
- B) That the strongest should rule
- C) That people are born free and equal
- D) That the land must never change

3. Why does the speaker say his words cannot "make this ground holy"?
- A) Because the ceremony is not important
- B) Because no one is listening to the speech
- C) Because the war is already over
- D) Because the soldiers' actions have already made it sacred

4. In the passage, "break apart" most nearly means
- A) grow larger
- B) split up and not survive
- C) come together
- D) change its name

5. The overall tone of the passage can best be described as
- A) solemn and inspiring
- B) angry and bitter
- C) light and playful
- D) cold and uninterested

**Answer Key**

1) **C** — Main idea. The closing turns to "us, the living," calling on them to finish the soldiers' unfinished work so that a "government of the people, by the people, for the people" survives.
2) **C** — Detail. The first paragraph says the nation rests on the belief "that everyone is born free and that all people are equal."
3) **D** — Inference. The text says the soldiers "have already made it sacred far more than we ever could," so it is their deeds, not the speaker's words, that make the ground holy.
4) **B** — Vocabulary in context. "Break apart" is set against "survive," so it means to split up and not last. the other options and D do not fit.
5) **A** — Tone / craft. The passage mourns the dead and urges the living forward; it is solemn yet inspiring, not playful, bitter or cold.
