## Overview

**Topic:** *A Future Without Work* — the text first lays out two gloomy visions, then pushes back. Adapted from a same-titled essay in *The Atlantic*.

**Answer key in one line (20 blanks):** `C A D A B B C A D C · C B A D C D A B D B`

**Line of argument:** Paragraphs 1–2 set up two pessimistic pictures of a work-free future (① wealth **inequality**; ② lives without **meaning**, people turning lazy and depressed). Paragraph 3 turns the argument with *necessarily / absence / yield*: that gloom only reflects being jobless **in a society built around employment** — designed differently, it needn't be so. The last paragraph, via the scholar Danaher, argues the **virtue** of work is overblown; freed from it, people would pour work-like intensity into hobbies.

> **Test tip:** In the phone practice screen, blank 1 was pre-highlighted on A (*boasting*) — that's a distractor. The key is **C. warning** (see analysis below).

## Full text with parallel translation (answers filled)

**① Paragraph 1**

People have speculated for centuries about a future without work. Today is no different, with academics, writers, and activists once again **(1) warning** that technology be replacing human workers. Some imagine that the coming work-free world will be defined by **(2) inequality**. A few wealthy people will own all the capital, and the masses will struggle in an impoverished wasteland.

> 几个世纪以来,人们一直在猜想一个没有工作的未来。今天也不例外——学者、作家和活动人士再次**(1) 警告**说,技术正在取代人类劳动者。有些人设想,即将到来的无工作世界将以**(2) 不平等**为特征:少数富人占有全部资本,而大众则在贫困的荒原中苦苦挣扎。

**② Paragraph 2**

A different and not mutually exclusive **(3) prediction** holds that the future will be a wasteland of a different sort, one **(4) characterized** by purposelessness: Without jobs to give their lives **(5) meaning**, people will simply become lazy and depressed. **(6) Indeed**, today's unemployed don't seem to be having a great time. One Gallup poll found that 20 percent of Americans who have been unemployed for at least a year report having depression, double the rate for **(7) working** Americans. Also, some research suggests that the **(8) explanation** for rising rates of mortality, mental-health problems, and addiction **(9) among** poorly-educated middle-aged people is shortage of well-paid jobs. Perhaps this is why many **(10) worry about** the agonizing dullness of a jobless future.

> 另一种与之并不互相排斥的**(3) 预测**则认为,未来将是另一种荒原——一种以**(4) 无目的感**为特征的荒原:没有工作来赋予生活**(5) 意义**,人们只会变得懒散而抑郁。**(6) 的确**,如今的失业者似乎过得并不好。盖洛普的一项民意调查发现,失业至少一年的美国人中有 20% 自述患有抑郁,是**(7) 在职**美国人的两倍。此外,一些研究表明,在受教育程度较低的中年人群**(9) 当中**,死亡率上升、心理健康问题与成瘾现象日益增多,其**(8) 原因**在于高薪岗位的短缺。也许正因如此,许多人才**(10) 担忧**那种没有工作的未来所带来的、令人痛苦的乏味。

**③ Paragraph 3**

But it doesn't **(11) necessarily** follow from findings like these that a world without work would be filled with unease. Such visions are based on the **(12) downsides** of being unemployed in a society built on the concept of employment. In the **(13) absence** of work, a society designed with other ends in mind could **(14) yield** strikingly different circumstances for the future of labor and leisure. Today, the **(15) virtue** of work may be a bit overblown. "Many jobs are boring, degrading, unhealthy, and a waste of human potential," says John Danaher, a lecturer at the National University of Ireland in Galway.

> 但是,由这类研究结果并不**(11) 必然**能得出「一个没有工作的世界必将充满不安」这一结论。这些悲观图景所依据的,不过是「在一个以就业为核心构建的社会里」失业所带来的种种**(12) 弊端**。而在**(13) 没有**工作的情况下,一个以其他目标来设计的社会,或许能为劳动与闲暇的未来**(14) 带来**截然不同的境况。如今,工作的**(15) 价值**也许有点被夸大了。爱尔兰国立大学戈尔韦分校的讲师约翰·丹纳赫说:「许多工作枯燥、有损尊严、有害健康,而且是对人类潜能的浪费。」

**④ Paragraph 4**

These days, because leisure time is relatively **(16) scarce** for most workers, people use their free time to counterbalance the intellectual and emotional **(17) demands** of their jobs. "When I come home from a hard day's work, I often feel **(18) tired**," Danaher says, adding, "In a world in which I don't have to work, I might feel rather different"—perhaps different enough to throw himself **(19) into** a hobby or a passion project with the intensity usually reserved for **(20) professional** matters.

> 如今,由于对大多数劳动者来说闲暇时间相对**(16) 稀缺**,人们便用空闲来平衡工作在智力和情感上的**(17) 消耗**。丹纳赫说:「辛苦工作一天回到家,我常常感到**(18) 疲惫**。」他还补充道:「在一个我不必工作的世界里,我的感受也许会大不相同」——或许不同到足以让他把往常只留给**(20) 工作**正事的那股劲头,全身心**(19) 投入**到某项爱好或热爱的事业中去。

## All 20 blanks explained (why the key)

**1. C — warning**
- *Sentence:* …activists once again **warning** that technology be replacing human workers.
- *✓ Why:* the whole passage sounds worried that tech is displacing people; *warn that…* fits.
- *✗ Not:* A boasting (opposite tone); B denying (contradicts "replacing is happening"); D ensuring (illogical).

**2. A — inequality**
- *Sentence:* …the coming work-free world will be defined by **inequality**.
- *✓ Why:* the next line — a few own all capital, the masses in poverty — is exactly a rich/poor split.
- *✗ Not:* B instability; C unreliability; D uncertainty — none carries the wealth-gap sense.

**3. D — prediction**
- *Sentence:* A different and not mutually exclusive **prediction** holds that the future will be a wasteland…
- *✓ Why:* it parallels the first vision, and only a "prediction" can *hold that the future will be…*.
- *✗ Not:* A policy; B guideline; C resolution — none forecasts the future.

**4. A — characterized**
- *Sentence:* …a wasteland of a different sort, one **characterized** by purposelessness.
- *✓ Why:* *be characterized by* = marked/defined by (fixed collocation); its feature is purposelessness.
- *✗ Not:* B divided by; C balanced by; D measured by — all illogical here.

**5. B — meaning**
- *Sentence:* Without jobs to give their lives **meaning**, people will simply become lazy and depressed.
- *✓ Why:* *give one's life meaning*; losing that sense of meaning is what makes people lazy/depressed.
- *✗ Not:* A wisdom; C glory; D freedom — none fits the cause→effect.

**6. B — Indeed**
- *Sentence:* **Indeed**, today's unemployed don't seem to be having a great time.
- *✓ Why:* this line backs up the previous claim with evidence; *Indeed* = confirming.
- *✗ Not:* A Instead (needs contrast); C Thus (this is evidence, not a cause→result); D Nevertheless (opposite).

**7. C — working**
- *Sentence:* …double the rate for **working** Americans.
- *✓ Why:* contrast with *unemployed* — the jobless rate is double that of "working" Americans.
- *✗ Not:* A rich; B urban; D educated — none is the direct opposite of "unemployed".

**8. A — explanation**
- *Sentence:* …the **explanation** for rising rates of mortality… is shortage of well-paid jobs.
- *✓ Why:* *the explanation for … is …* = the reason for … is …; the cause is spelled out after "is".
- *✗ Not:* B requirement; C compensation; D substitute — none fits "the ___ for … is a shortage".

**9. D — among**
- *Sentence:* …and addiction **among** poorly-educated middle-aged people…
- *✓ Why:* *among* = within a group; the addiction is within that population.
- *✗ Not:* A under; B beyond; C alongside — none means "within a group".

**10. C — worry about**
- *Sentence:* Perhaps this is why many **worry about** the agonizing dullness of a jobless future.
- *✓ Why:* following all the negatives, people "worry about" the dreary jobless future.
- *✗ Not:* A leave behind; B make up; D set aside — all semantically off.

**11. C — necessarily**
- *Sentence:* But it doesn't **necessarily** follow from findings like these that…
- *✓ Why:* *it doesn't necessarily follow that …* is a fixed frame, and the hinge of the whole argument.
- *✗ Not:* A statistically; B occasionally; D economically — none conveys "doesn't automatically follow".

**12. B — downsides**
- *Sentence:* Such visions are based on the **downsides** of being unemployed…
- *✓ Why:* the gloom rests on the drawbacks of joblessness *in a job-centered society*; the text then rebuts it.
- *✗ Not:* A chances; C benefits (opposite); D principles — none fits "the misery of unemployment".

**13. A — absence**
- *Sentence:* In the **absence** of work, a society designed with other ends in mind…
- *✓ Why:* *in the absence of* = without (fixed phrase); pairs with "a society with other ends".
- *✗ Not:* B height (in the height of); C face (in the face of); D course (in the course of) — none fits.

**14. D — yield**
- *Sentence:* …could **yield** strikingly different circumstances for the future of labor and leisure.
- *✓ Why:* *yield* = produce/bring about; a different design could bring about very different conditions.
- *✗ Not:* A disturb; B restore; C exclude — none means "produce new conditions".

**15. C — virtue**
- *Sentence:* Today, the **virtue** of work may be a bit overblown.
- *✓ Why:* *the virtue of work* = its value/merit; Danaher's next line (jobs are boring, degrading…) deflates it.
- *✗ Not:* A model; B practice — don't pair with *overblown*; D hardship — if hardship were overblown, the quote should downplay it, but it stresses how bad jobs are (contradiction).

**16. D — scarce**
- *Sentence:* …because leisure time is relatively **scarce** for most workers…
- *✓ Why:* precisely because free time is scarce, people spend it offsetting work's toll.
- *✗ Not:* A tricky; B lengthy (opposite of scarce); C mysterious — none fits.

**17. A — demands**
- *Sentence:* …counterbalance the intellectual and emotional **demands** of their jobs.
- *✓ Why:* *demands* = the intellectual/emotional pressures a job imposes, which leisure offsets.
- *✗ Not:* B standards; C qualities; D threats — none pairs with *counterbalance*.

**18. B — tired**
- *Sentence:* When I come home from a hard day's work, I often feel **tired**.
- *✓ Why:* after a hard day → tired; it contrasts with "in a world where I don't have to work, I'd feel different".
- *✗ Not:* A ignored; C confused; D starved — none fits "a hard day's work".

**19. D — into**
- *Sentence:* …different enough to throw himself **into** a hobby or a passion project…
- *✓ Why:* *throw oneself into* = commit fully (fixed collocation).
- *✗ Not:* A off; B against; C behind — none forms the "commit to" sense.

**20. B — professional**
- *Sentence:* …with the intensity usually reserved for **professional** matters.
- *✓ Why:* that intensity is normally saved for work; freed from it, he'd aim it at hobbies — a work-vs-hobby contrast.
- *✗ Not:* A technological; C educational; D interpersonal — none fits the contrast.

> **Method recap:** the four-step cloze routine — ① read the first sentence for tone and grasp the whole; ② read the **logic** between the gap's clause and its neighbors (contrast / evidence / comparison / cause); ③ lock it with **collocations** (characterized by / in the absence of / throw oneself into / it doesn't necessarily follow); ④ **eliminate** near-synonym distractors by sense.
