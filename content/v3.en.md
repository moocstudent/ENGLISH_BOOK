## Core Notes

### Register: spoken vs written

**Register** is how formal your language is; it shifts with audience, purpose, and medium.

| Informal | Neutral | Formal |
|---|---|---|
| kids | children | minors |
| get | receive | acquire |
| ask for | request | solicit |
| buy | purchase | procure |

Match the situation — more formal is not always better. "I'd like your feedback" beats "I would like to solicit your feedback" among colleagues.

### Comparing synonyms

Near-synonyms rarely swap freely. Check four things:

1. **Collocation:** a **big** mistake ✓, a *large* mistake ✗.
2. **Register:** ask < request < inquire.
3. **Connotation:** *slim* (positive) vs *skinny* (negative) vs *thin* (neutral).
4. **Grammar:** *tell me* but *say to me*.

Look up examples, not just a Chinese gloss.

### Academic Word List (AWL)

About 570 word families common in academic texts:

| Everyday | Academic |
|---|---|
| show | demonstrate, indicate |
| use | utilize, employ |
| enough | sufficient |
| but | however |
| so | therefore |

They add precision, but keep the right collocations (**conduct** research) and don't swap every word.

### Avoid pompous word stacking

Big words are not automatically better.

- ✗ *I utilize this methodology to facilitate my cognition.*
- ✓ *I use this method to help me understand.*

Cut redundancy (*free gift, past history*). Choose **clarity over flash**.

### Rewriting for register

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| Informal | Can you get back to me soon? |
| Neutral | Please reply when you can. |
| Formal | I would appreciate your response soon. |

To go up: phrasal → single verb, add polite frames, drop contractions. To go down: reverse it.

### Summary

Match register to the situation; compare synonyms by collocation, register, connotation, and grammar; use AWL words with care.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Formal email: ask for / request / get more information → **request**

**Example 2.** Fix: *a large mistake* → **a big mistake**

**Example 3.** Translate (academic): The data **indicate** the method is **sufficiently** effective.

**Example 4.** Positive word for a slim friend: slim / skinny / thin → **slim**

**Example 5.** Make informal: "I would appreciate your response." → *Let me know when you get a chance!*

## Exercises

1. Order by formality: get, obtain, acquire.
2. a ___ mistake (big / large).
3. Academic swaps for show, use, but.
4. Formalize: *Can you get back to me?*
5. Is *skinny* positive or negative?
6. Fix: *I utilize a spoon to eat.*

**Answer key.** 1) get < obtain < acquire  2) big  3) demonstrate/indicate; utilize/employ; however  4) I would appreciate your reply.  5) negative  6) use.
