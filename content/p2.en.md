## Core Notes

### Fluency vs accuracy

**Fluency** = keep talking smoothly; **accuracy** = correct grammar. B1–B2 speakers often freeze chasing zero errors and go silent, which is harder to follow. When speaking, keep the flow and finish your idea; fix small slips lightly.

| | Fluency-first | Accuracy-first |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | be understood | be correct |
| Best for | chat, discussion | writing, grammar tests |
| Stuck? | talk around it | stop and fix |

Communication > perfection.

### Fillers & discourse markers

Buy thinking time with **natural fillers**, not a long "uhhh":

| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| Buy time | *Well… / Let me think… / Good question…* |
| Soften | *you know / I mean / kind of* |
| Organize | *So… / First of all… / Anyway…* |
| Signal | *Actually… / To be honest… / The thing is…* |

Use them lightly — a *you know* in every clause distracts.

### Self-repair

- **Correct yourself:** *Sorry, I mean… / What I'm trying to say is… / Let me rephrase.*
- **Paraphrase** around an unknown word:
  - *It's a thing you use to…*
  - *It's a kind of…*, *the opposite of…*, *someone who…*
- Forgot *thermometer*? Say *the thing that measures temperature.*
- Don't get stuck on one word — talk around it.

### Expanding your answers

Beat one-word replies with **answer + reason + example**, then bounce it back:

- ✗ *Do you like your job?* — *Yes.*
- ✓ *Yeah, I do — mainly because of the team. Last week we… What about you?*

Add WH-info (who / when / where / why) to fill out the idea.

### Everyday task

**30-second monologue — a hobby:**

> *One thing I really enjoy is hiking. I got into it a couple of years ago because I wanted less screen time. What I love is being outdoors and switching off. Last month I hiked a small mountain — tough but worth it. How about you?*

Record → replay → find long pauses and stuck words → redo with fillers and paraphrase. Shadow native audio for rhythm.

### Summary

Fluency before accuracy; fill pauses naturally; paraphrase unknown words; expand with reason + example; record, replay, redo.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Unprepared question → *That's a good question… let me think for a second.*

**Example 2.** Expand *Yes* (like coffee?) → *Yeah — a cup every morning to wake up. You?*

**Example 3.** Paraphrase *stapler* → *the little thing you use to join papers.*

**Example 4.** Self-repair: *We'll meet Tuesday — sorry, I mean Thursday.*

**Example 5.** Expand *The project is fine.* → *It's going well — on schedule, client liked the draft; only testing's left.*

## Exercises

1. Give three time-buying fillers.
2. Expand *No* (watch sports?) into a 20-second answer.
3. Paraphrase *microwave* without saying the word.
4. Fix: *I want say another thing.* → **I want to add one more thing.**
5. True/False: adding *you know* everywhere makes you fluent. → **False**
6. Answer "What do you do at weekends?" with answer + reason + example + bounce-back.

**Answer key.** 1/2/3/6 model answers vary; 4 above; 5 False.
