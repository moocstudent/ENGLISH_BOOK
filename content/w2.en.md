## Core Notes

### Argumentative vs expository

| Aspect | Expository | Argumentative |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | inform | persuade |
| Stance | neutral | takes a side |
| Ending | summarise facts | restate position |

An argument = **claim + evidence + handling the other side**, like a polite debate.

### Intro: hook + thesis

- **Hook:** a question, a scenario, or a trend statement (never invent fake data).
- **Thesis:** one sentence giving your **position + main reasons**.

> Weak: *Pollution is bad.*
> Strong: *Cities should limit private cars, because this cuts pollution and improves public transport.*

### Body paragraphs (PEEL)

- **Point** — the sub-claim
- **Evidence** — reasons, everyday or hypothetical examples, logic
- **Explanation** — how the evidence supports the point
- **Link** — close or transition

At B1–B2, common sense and hypothetical examples are fine; you don't need statistics.

### Counterargument

Concede, then rebut:

| Stage | Example |
|---|---|
| Concede | It is true that free tuition is expensive. |
| Rebut | However, an educated workforce repays that cost. |

Avoid fallacies: hasty generalisation, false dilemma, slippery slope, ad hominem.

### IELTS / TOEFL Task 2

| Prompt type | Shape |
|---|---|
| Opinion | clear stance + two support paragraphs |
| Discussion | one view + other view + your opinion |
| Adv/Disadv | pros + cons + verdict |
| Problem/Solution | problem + solution |

Four to five paragraphs; IELTS needs 250+ words. The conclusion **restates** the thesis in new words — it never copies the intro.

### Summary

Take a clear stance, prove it with PEEL bodies, answer the other side, and restate (not repeat) in the conclusion.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Thesis: *Schools should ban phones in lessons, because they distract students and weaken social skills.*

**Example 2.** Hook upgrade: *Technology is important* → *Imagine losing your phone for a week — no maps, no messages, no payments.*

**Example 3.** Fill in: *___ online learning is convenient, it cannot replace a real classroom.* → **Although**

**Example 4.** Order ② ④ ① ③ = Point → Explain → Example → Link.

**Example 5.** A conclusion that copies the intro is weak; restate in new words and add a recommendation.

## Exercises

1. One key difference between expository and argumentative writing?
2. Is *Pollution is bad* a strong thesis? Why not?
3. Write one concede-then-rebut sentence.
4. Name the fallacy: *Allow phones and students will stop reading entirely.* → **slippery slope**
5. How is the body arranged for a "discuss both views" task?
6. What should a conclusion do, and not do?

**Answer key.** (4) slippery slope; (2) too vague — no position or reason; (5) one paragraph per view plus your opinion; (6) restate and recommend, don't copy the intro; (1) and (3) self-check.
