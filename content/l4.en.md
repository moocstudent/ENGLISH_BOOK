## Core Notes

### The Cornell method

Split the page into three zones:

| Zone | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| Right (wide) | while listening | keywords, points, numbers |
| Left (narrow) | after | cues, questions, labels |
| Bottom strip | after | one or two summary lines |

It separates **capture** (during) from **processing** (after) — while listening, just grab.

### Keywords only

You can't write as fast as people talk, so don't transcribe.

- **Keep:** nouns, numbers, names, signal words, the "so what".
- **Drop:** articles, tense detail, fillers.
- "Costs rose by 20% in 2023" → note "costs ↑20% (2023)".

### Listening vs writing

Writing competes with listening for attention — write too much and you miss the next point.

- **Rule: listening first.** Three words that catch the idea beat a full sentence that makes you miss what follows.
- Use the speaker's pauses and signposts as **writing windows** — note in bursts, not continuously.

### A symbol system

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| → | leads to, result |
| ↑ ↓ | up / down |
| = | is, means |
| ∴ | therefore |
| ∵ | because |
| eg | for example |
| impt | important |

Abbreviate by dropping vowels (mgmt), taking the first syllable (info, dev), or using standard forms (dept, yr, hr). **Be consistent** — shorthand only works if you always use the same set.

### Review after listening

Expand keywords into full points while they're fresh; fill gaps from memory and mark what to re-listen. Add the left-column cues and the bottom summary. Then **retell from your notes aloud** — it tests understanding, not just hearing.

### Mock exam practice

- **Read the questions first** (if allowed); note only what answers them.
- **Don't freeze on a missed answer** — let it go and keep pace with the recording.
- **Leave checking time** (e.g. the IELTS transfer window) for spelling and grammar.
- Practise under timed, one-play or two-play conditions.

### Summary

Cornell zones; keywords plus signposts; listening beats writing; a steady symbol set; review and retell after.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** Compress: "Sales dropped sharply last quarter because of supply problems." → **sales ↓↓ last Q ∵ supply probs**

**Example 2.** Symbols: "Demand rose, so prices increased." → **demand ↑ → price ↑**

**Example 3.** Expand: "mtg w/ mgmt re budget @ 3, impt" → **Meeting with management about the budget at 3 — important.**

**Example 4.** Listening vs writing: the speaker is fast and you want every sentence. → **Switch to listening-first**: 2–3 keywords per point, top up at pauses.

**Example 5.** Cornell template: right (while listening) keywords / left (after) cue questions / bottom one-line summary.

## Exercises

1. Name the three Cornell zones — what and when for each?
2. Which word types do you keep vs drop when noting?
3. Why is "listening > writing"?
4. Rewrite with symbols: "costs fell because sales dropped".
5. Expand: "info dept, yr-end report, cf last yr".
6. You miss one answer in a listening exam — what do you do?

**Answer key.**

1) Right/wide (while listening): keywords; left/narrow (after): cues/questions; bottom (after): summary lines. 2) Keep nouns, numbers, names, signal words, conclusions; drop articles, tense detail, fillers. 3) Writing competes with listening; too much and you miss the next point. 4) costs ↓ ∵ sales ↓. 5) Information department, year-end report, compare with last year. 6) Let it go, keep pace with the recording, recover it afterwards.
