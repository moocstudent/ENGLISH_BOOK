## Core Notes

### Format and scoring points

| | English I | English II |
|---|---|---|
| Form | 5 underlined sentences | one paragraph (~150 words) |
| Points | 10 (2 each) | 15 |

Graded by **scoring segments**: each sentence is cut into points (main clause + modifiers); render a segment right and you earn that share, even if the whole is imperfect. Two rules: **accuracy before elegance**, and **never leave a blank** — an empty answer scores zero.

### Parsing complex sentences

Split, then rebuild. Steps: (1) find the verb and the main **S–V–O**; (2) cut out clauses and phrases (*that/which/who*, participles, prepositional phrases); (3) see what each modifies; (4) reorder for Chinese — modifiers before nouns, adverbials early, long sentences broken up; (5) recombine and re-read for omissions. Cut at relative words, conjunctions, punctuation, prepositions.

### Relative clauses: pre- vs post-positioning

- **Short clause → pre-position** as "…的 + noun": *the book that I bought* → 我买的那本书.
- **Long clause → post-position**, as a new clause repeating the antecedent: *a method, which cut the cost* → …一种方法,这种方法降低了成本.

Non-restrictive (comma) clauses usually go post-position as added information.

### Passive, nominalization, meaning extension

- **Passive:** Chinese avoids "被". Turn it active (*Mistakes were made* → 有人犯了错), make it subjectless (*Measures must be taken* → 必须采取措施), or use 受/由 (*was affected by* → 受……影响).
- **Nominalization → verb:** *the destruction of the forest* → 森林遭到破坏; *the rise in prices* → 物价上涨.
- **Meaning extension:** adjust by context — *a heavy smoker* → 烟瘾很大的人, not "heavy".

### Sentence-by-sentence practice

Five steps per sentence: main clause → modifiers → clause method → passive/nominalization → recombine and check the scoring points. *Scientists who study sleep have found that the brain, far from resting, remains active at night.* → 研究睡眠的科学家发现,大脑非但没有休息,夜里反而依然活跃。

### Summary

Scored by segments — accurate and complete first, never blank. Cut the main clause out; short relatives pre-position, long ones post-position; passives go active or subjectless; nominalizations become verbs.

## Worked Examples

**Example 1.** *The report, which was released last week, warns that the plan may fail.* → 上周发布的这份报告警告说,该计划可能会失败。

**Example 2.** *She proposed a solution that no one had thought of before.* → 她提出了一个此前无人想到的方案。

**Example 3.** *New rules were introduced to protect consumers.* → 为保护消费者,(有关部门)出台了新规。 (subjectless, not "were introduced")

**Example 4.** *The wide application of AI has changed how we work.* → 人工智能的广泛应用改变了我们的工作方式。 (noun → verb)

**Example 5.** *What surprised the researchers was that the effect, though small, lasted for years.* → 令研究者惊讶的是,这种效应尽管微小,却持续了数年。
