## Passage

*— Adapted from The Adventure of the Speckled Band, Arthur Conan Doyle (1892). A public-domain work.*

It was early in April when Holmes woke me one grey morning, already dressed and clearly eager.

"I am sorry to disturb you, Watson," he said, "but a young lady has arrived in great distress and refuses to wait. When a woman travels across the city at such an hour and rouses strangers from their sleep, the matter is surely urgent."

We went down together. A woman in black rose to meet us, her face pale behind a thin veil. She was young, yet her hair was already touched with grey, and her eyes moved about the room like those of a hunted animal.

"You must not be frightened," Holmes said kindly. "We shall soon put things right. You have come up by train this morning, I think."

She stared at him. "How could you know that?"

"It is simple enough. The return half of your ticket is peeping from your left glove. You started before dawn, and you had a rough ride in a dog-cart along muddy lanes before you reached the station."

The lady gave a sharp start and gazed at him in astonishment.

"There is no magic in it," he smiled. "The left sleeve of your jacket is spattered with fresh mud in seven places. Only a dog-cart throws up mud in that way, and only upon a passenger seated to the driver's left."

Once again I marvelled, as I had so often before, at the quiet ease of his reasoning.

## Notes

- **Source / author:** "The Adventure of the Speckled Band" (1892) is one of the most famous Sherlock Holmes short stories by the British writer Arthur Conan Doyle, collected in *The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes*. This passage is an adaptation, told in Watson's voice, of the scene in which a frightened young woman calls at Baker Street at dawn — not the original text.
- **Main idea:** Through Holmes's observation of small details — a train ticket, spots of mud — the passage shows detective reasoning that moves "from detail to conclusion," and his calm, sharp powers of observation.
- **Structure / craft:** A first-person narrative that first sets up suspense (the woman's fear), then reveals the chain of reasoning through dialogue; every conclusion is tied to visible evidence (the return ticket in her left glove, seven spots of mud on her sleeve).
- **Tone:** Tense yet composed; the frightened lady and the gentle, confident Holmes stand in contrast.

## Reading Comprehension

1. According to Holmes, how did the young lady travel to the station before catching her train?
- A) On foot
- B) By dog-cart
- C) In her own private carriage
- D) On horseback

2. What first told Holmes that she had arrived by train?
- A) Her muddy shoes
- B) The return half of a ticket in her left glove
- C) She told him so at once
- D) The early hour of her visit

3. What can we infer about the young woman's state of mind?
- A) She is calm and cheerful.
- B) She is deeply frightened.
- C) She is angry with Holmes.
- D) She is only mildly curious.

4. In "The lady gave a sharp start," the word *start* most nearly means
- A) a beginning
- B) a sudden movement of surprise
- C) a loud cry
- D) a slow step

5. How does Holmes mainly reach his conclusions?
- A) By guessing luckily
- B) By asking the lady many questions
- C) By closely observing small details
- D) By reading her private letters

**Answer Key**

1) **B** — Detail. Holmes says she "had a rough ride in a dog-cart along muddy lanes before you reached the station," so the answer is B.
2) **B** — Detail. The text states plainly: "The return half of your ticket is peeping from your left glove." This is the first clue that told him she came by train.
3) **B** — Inference. She arrives "in great distress," her hair has turned grey early, and her eyes move "like those of a hunted animal" — all signs of deep fear, so B.
4) **B** — Vocabulary in context. Here *start* is a noun meaning a sudden jump of surprise; the next line, "gazed at him in astonishment," confirms surprise rather than "a beginning."
5) **C** — Main idea / craft. Each of Holmes's conclusions rests on a visible detail (the ticket in the glove, seven mud spots on the sleeve), showing close observation rather than luck or questioning, so C.
